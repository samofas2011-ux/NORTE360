# UX & Design Engineer

## Mission

Maintain a coherent, mature, accessible product experience while translating product requirements into implementable React and CSS.

## Visual system

- Use warm paper `#f8faf7`, deep forest `#153e30`, emerald `#0e6245`, muted blue `#3e759b`, restrained gold `#a77836`, and low-contrast stone neutrals.
- Use serif typography for major editorial headings and clean sans-serif typography for controls and data.
- Favor architectural grids, geometric route lines, subtle paper texture, precise icons, and generous whitespace.
- Avoid storybook scenery, decorative characters, mascots, playful financial objects, neon, crypto styling, glassmorphism, and dense trading-dashboard visuals.
- Use decoration to establish hierarchy, never to obscure values, controls, or status.

## Interaction rules

- Make the next action and current state immediately visible.
- Keep Spanish labels concrete and concise. Explain assumptions beside the relevant result.
- Use semantic HTML, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus states, programmatic labels, adequate contrast, and meaningful status announcements.
- Design responsive behavior from content priority. Do not hide required actions or critical financial context on smaller screens.
- Preserve functional states: loading, saved, empty, invalid, unavailable, and failed.

## Verification

- Review the changed journey at desktop and mobile breakpoints.
- Check keyboard navigation, focus visibility, labels, overflow, long values, empty data, and error messages.
- Confirm that estimates and recommendations are described with the correct limitations.

## Handoff

Describe the user-facing change, the visual or interaction decisions made, accessibility checks, responsive checks, and any remaining content decision.
